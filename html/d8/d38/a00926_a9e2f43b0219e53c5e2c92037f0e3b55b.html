◆ USUnit

enum SystemEx.Numeric.USUnit

Defines the set of supported U.S. customary engineering units used for formatting and interpreting BigDecimal values. These units represent common American measurement systems frequently encountered in mechanical, civil, automotive, industrial, and HVAC engineering.

Enumerator
Inch 

Inch (in). A fundamental U.S. length unit widely used in manufacturing, machining, construction, and mechanical engineering.

Foot 

Foot (ft). Equal to 12 inches. Commonly used in construction, surveying, architecture, and general engineering applications.

Yard 

Yard (yd). Equal to 3 feet or 36 inches. Used in textiles, manufacturing, and certain civil engineering contexts.

Mile 

Mile (mi). A large-scale U.S. length unit used for geographic distances, transportation engineering, and navigation.

Ounce 

Ounce (oz). A small U.S. mass unit used in everyday measurements, packaging, and lightweight material specifications.

Pound 

Pound (lb). A standard U.S. mass unit equal to 16 ounces. Widely used in mechanical engineering, material science, and industrial applications.

Cup 

Cup. A U.S. volume unit equal to 8 fluid ounces. Commonly used in food science, chemistry labs, and consumer product measurements.

Pint 

Pint (pt). A U.S. volume unit equal to 16 fluid ounces. Used in food production, chemical mixtures, and fluid handling.

Quart 

Quart (qt). A U.S. volume unit equal to 32 fluid ounces or one quarter of a gallon. Used in automotive fluids, chemical processing, and food manufacturing.

Gallon 

Gallon (gal). A large U.S. volume unit equal to 128 fluid ounces. Common in automotive engineering, HVAC systems, fuel measurement, and industrial fluid handling.

PSI 

PSI (pounds per square inch). A standard U.S. pressure unit widely used in automotive engineering, hydraulics, pneumatics, and industrial systems.

BTU 

BTU (British Thermal Unit). A U.S. energy unit used extensively in HVAC, heating systems, thermodynamics, and energy engineering.

Horsepower 

Horsepower (hp). A U.S. power unit used in automotive engineering, mechanical systems, and industrial machinery performance ratings.
